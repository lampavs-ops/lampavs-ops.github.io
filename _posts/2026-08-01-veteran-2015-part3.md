## 🎬 A Director Who Keeps His Crew Close

Ryoo Seung-wan has a reputation in the Korean film industry for working with the same actors and crew members again and again, film after film. Hwang Jung-min and Yoo Hae-jin had both worked with him on earlier projects before Veteran, and that familiarity shows up on screen as genuine chemistry, not just good casting. Ryoo has cited buddy-cop franchises like Lethal Weapon as an inspiration for this approach, where returning faces build a sense of a real team over time instead of a one-off ensemble assembled for a single story.

## 🎭 Playing Against Type

Casting Yoo Ah-in as the reckless, violent chaebol heir Jo Tae-oh was a deliberate change of pace. At the time, Yoo Ah-in was better known for quieter, more introspective roles. Taking on a character defined by explosive temper and casual cruelty pushed him into new territory as an actor, and the performance is often cited as one of the most memorable villain turns in modern Korean cinema, precisely because it felt like such a departure from what audiences expected of him.
