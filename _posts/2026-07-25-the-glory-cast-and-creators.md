## ✍️ A Writer Known for Going Big

Kim Eun-sook has one of the most recognizable names in Korean television writing. Before The Glory, she wrote Descendants of the Sun, a military romance that became a massive regional hit across Asia, and Goblin, a fantasy romance widely considered one of the most beloved K-dramas of its decade. Both of those shows lean warm and romantic. The Glory is almost the opposite: cold, patient, and built around a slow-burn revenge plot instead of a love story. Seeing the same writer succeed at both extremes is part of why she is considered one of the most versatile names working in Korean television today.

## 🌟 The Villain Everyone Talked About

Lim Ji-yeon's performance as Park Yeon-jin became a genuine breakout moment. Playing a character audiences are supposed to despise is a difficult balancing act — too cartoonish and nobody takes the threat seriously, too subtle and the character's cruelty does not land. Lim found a middle ground that made Park Yeon-jin feel unsettlingly real, and her Best Supporting Actress win at the Baeksang Arts Awards, alongside Song Hye-kyo's Best Actress win for the same show, meant the two lead performances were recognized together at Korean television's biggest awards ceremony.

## 🎬 A Director Who Lets Silence Do the Work

Ahn Gil-ho does not have Kim Eun-sook's name recognition, but his fingerprints are all over why *The Glory* feels so different from a typical revenge melodrama. His camera lingers. Scenes that a faster-paced drama would cut after a few seconds, Ahn holds for a beat longer than feels comfortable — long enough for the audience to sit in Moon Dong-eun's stillness rather than have her explain what she is feeling. That patience is a directing choice as much as a writing one, and it is a large part of why the show reads as cold and controlled rather than melodramatic, even though the underlying story is about bullying, trauma, and revenge — subject matter that could easily tip into exaggeration in less careful hands.

## 🔄 Song Hye-kyo's Deliberate Image Break

It is hard to overstate how big a swing this role was for Song Hye-kyo. For roughly two decades, she had built a career on warmth — the kind of leading lady Korean audiences associated with gentleness, tears, and quiet devotion. Moon Dong-eun asked her to strip nearly all of that away: flat affect, careful stillness, a face that reveals almost nothing until the story chooses to let it. Actors who built a career on one register do not always survive a hard pivot away from it. Song's performance is frequently cited as proof she could, and it reopened her career to a completely different kind of role going forward.

## 🎭 Where the Cast Went Next

Success on a show this large tends to open doors immediately, and *The Glory*'s cast is a good example. Lim Ji-yeon went on to lead further high-profile projects on the strength of Park Yeon-jin's reception. Park Sung-hoon, who played Jeon Jae-jun, followed his turn here with a role in *Squid Game* Season 2 — a jump from one of Netflix's biggest Korean hits to another. Lee Do-hyun continued building a leading-man résumé across both film and television. For a cast that was mostly known before this show, *The Glory* functioned less like one project and more like a launching pad.

## 🧩 Why This Casting Worked

Revenge dramas live or die on one thing: does the audience actually believe the people inside the plan? Casting an actress known for warmth as the coldest character on Korean television that year was a risk precisely because it worked against type — but that mismatch is exactly what made Moon Dong-eun's stillness read as *earned* rather than written. Pairing her against Lim Ji-yeon, an actress with far less built-in audience goodwill to lose, let Park Yeon-jin be genuinely, uncomplicatedly hateable without the show needing to soften her for the sake of a familiar face. That contrast — a beloved actress playing ice, an equally skilled but less iconic actress playing fire — is a big part of why the two performances landed as a pair rather than competing for the same spotlight.
