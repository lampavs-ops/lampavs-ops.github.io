## Behind the Camera

### Yoo In-shik — Director

Yoo In-shik has been directing Korean television since the early 2000s, most of that time as an in-house producer at SBS before going freelance. His resume before *Extraordinary Attorney Woo* already included some of the more distinctive dramas of the last fifteen years: the sprawling family saga *Giant*, the globe-trotting thriller *Vagabond*, and all three seasons of the medical drama *Dr. Romantic*.

*Extraordinary Attorney Woo* was something different for him — smaller in scale, gentler in tone, built around a character type Korean television had barely attempted. In a 2023 interview with Forbes, he said the show's biggest challenge was balance: finding the line between humor and mockery, between old drama clichés and a genuinely new kind of story, without the compromise feeling forced in either direction. He also said the whale motif — the show's signature visual — wasn't in the original script at all. He added it himself, specifically because he wanted something only Young-woo and the audience could see, as a way of putting viewers directly inside her perspective.

After *Attorney Woo*, Yoo directed *Dr. Romantic 3* and then *The WONDERfools*, a Netflix original that reunited him with Park Eun-bin. He's since moved into new territory: a tvN spy romance titled *100 Days of Lies*, set for release in October 2026.

We couldn't confirm a public personal social media account for Yoo In-shik. If you want to follow his work, the most reliable path is the official pages for his dramas and production companies.

### Moon Ji-won — Writer

Moon Ji-won is the screenwriter who created Woo Young-woo. Before this show, she was best known in film circles for *Innocent Witness*, a courtroom drama about an autistic teenage witness that won the Grand Prize at the 2016 Lotte Scenario Contest — a blind competition judged without knowing the writers' names, out of nearly a thousand entries. That script's themes clearly carried forward into her next major project.

At the *Attorney Woo* press conference, Moon described what "strange" (이상한) means to her as a title: unusual, unfamiliar, distinctive people who sometimes make others uneasy, but who also push the world forward and make it more interesting.

**A correction worth flagging:** several fan sites and aggregators list an Instagram account for "Moon Ji-won" in connection with this show. We checked it directly — it belongs to a different person entirely, a reality-dating-show cast member who happens to share the same name. We're not linking it here, and if you see it attributed to the screenwriter elsewhere, that attribution is wrong.

## The Cast

### Park Eun-bin — Woo Young-woo

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@eunbining0904](https://www.instagram.com/eunbining0904/)

Park Eun-bin has been acting since childhood, but *Extraordinary Attorney Woo* was the role that turned her into one of Korea's most decorated performers overnight. At the 59th Baeksang Arts Awards in 2023, she won the Television Grand Prize, one of the ceremony's two highest honors (the other going to the winner in film). In interviews after the show ended, she said she deliberately avoided watching footage of real autistic people or previous autistic characters on screen, calling it an ethical line she didn't want to cross as an actor who isn't autistic herself. Instead, she built Young-woo from the script and the clinical diagnostic criteria for autism spectrum disorder.

Since the show wrapped, she has starred in *Castaway Diva*, the psychological medical thriller *Hyper Knife*, and *The WONDERfools*, reuniting her with director Yoo In-shik.

### Kang Tae-oh — Lee Jun-ho

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@kto940620](https://www.instagram.com/kto940620/)

Kang Tae-oh plays Lee Jun-ho, the Hanbada colleague who becomes Young-woo's boyfriend over the course of the season — patient, steady, and entirely unbothered by office gossip about the relationship. Before this show, Kang was already a familiar face from *Run On*, but *Attorney Woo* significantly widened his international audience, particularly across Southeast Asia.

### Kang Ki-young — Jung Myung-seok

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@booboo2injo](https://www.instagram.com/booboo2injo/)

Kang Ki-young plays Attorney Jung, Young-woo's gruff, big-hearted mentor at Hanbada — a role that led fans to nickname him Young-woo's "sub-dad" for how protectively he treats her despite constant grumbling. Kang built his career on comic supporting roles in dramas like *Oh My Ghost* and *Because This Is My First Life* long before this show, but Jung Myung-seok became the role that made him a lead actor in his own right: in 2024 he headlined JTBC's *Queen of Divorce* in his first starring part after fifteen years of acting.

### Ha Yoon-kyung — Choi Soo-yeon

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@hayoonkie](https://www.instagram.com/hayoonkie/)

Ha Yoon-kyung plays Choi Soo-yeon, Young-woo's law school classmate and the colleague most consistently in her corner at Hanbada — the one who says the uncomfortable, necessary thing out loud when nobody else will. It's a warm, grounded performance in a show that needed at least one character functioning as a straightforward ally, and Ha's timing carries a lot of the show's lighter workplace comedy.

### Joo Jong-hyuk — Kwon Min-woo

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@joojong_](https://www.instagram.com/joojong_/)

Joo Jong-hyuk plays Kwon Min-woo, the rookie attorney whose resentment toward Young-woo drives a good chunk of the show's workplace conflict. It's a genuinely difficult role — the character is written to be aggravating for most of sixteen episodes — and Joo has spoken about deliberately trying to find the insecurity underneath Min-woo's behavior rather than playing him as simply nasty. It paid off: the character became one of the most-discussed supporting roles of 2022, for better and worse.

### Joo Hyun-young — Dong Geu-ra-mi

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@2ruka__](https://www.instagram.com/2ruka__/)

Joo Hyun-young plays Dong Geu-ra-mi, Young-woo's one and only childhood best friend — loud, blunt, and fiercely protective since their school days together. Joo built a following through Korean sketch comedy before this role, and she brings that same fearless comic energy to Geu-ra-mi, who gets some of the show's biggest laughs and its warmest scenes in roughly equal measure.

### Jeon Bae-soo — Woo Gwang-ho

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@baesooaa](https://www.instagram.com/baesooaa/)

Jeon Bae-soo plays Woo Gwang-ho, Young-woo's father — a man who gave up his own path in law to raise his daughter alone after her mother left. He's a veteran character actor with decades of film and television credits, and the quiet, patient warmth he brings to Gwang-ho is a big part of why the father-daughter relationship anchors the whole show rather than feeling like a backstory footnote.

### Jin Kyung — Tae Soo-mi

> 📸 **Official Instagram:** [@jinkyung_actress](https://www.instagram.com/jinkyung_actress/)

Jin Kyung plays Tae Soo-mi, the Taesan attorney revealed to be Young-woo's biological mother. It's a role that demands real range — ambitious and cold in the courtroom, quietly devastated the moment her daughter's identity becomes real to her — and Jin threads that needle without ever making Soo-mi feel like two separate characters stitched together. She'd already worked with director Yoo In-shik on the first two seasons of *Dr. Romantic* before this show, and reunited with him again for *Dr. Romantic 3* afterward.

### Baek Ji-won — Han Seon-young

Baek Ji-won plays Han Seon-young, the composed, sharp-eyed CEO of Hanbada who makes the call to hire Young-woo. A prolific character actress, Baek has appeared in an unusually high volume of Korean dramas and films over the past decade, including *Snowdrop* and, more recently, *When Life Gives You Tangerines*. We weren't able to confirm a personal social media account for her — if you'd like to follow her work, her agency's official channels are the more reliable option.

## Wrapping Up the Series

That's *Extraordinary Attorney Woo*, start to finish: a breakout hit that rose from 0.9 percent to 17.5 percent and set a new ratings record for ENA, a hackberry tree that was officially designated a Korean Natural Monument, and a cast that turned a legal drama into something considerably warmer than the genre usually allows.
