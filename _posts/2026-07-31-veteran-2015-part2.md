## Quick Recap

In Part 1, we met **Seo Do-cheol**, a tough detective, and **Jo Tae-oh**, a spoiled chaebol heir. Do-cheol starts digging into Tae-oh after a truck driver named Bae gets badly hurt. Now, let's talk about how this story really ends.

---

## How the Story Unfolds

### A Disguise That Sets the Tone

Veteran opens with Do-cheol pretending to be a rich businessman. He is not really shopping for a car. He is undercover, working to catch a car smuggling ring. His "wife" for the scene is his teammate, Miss Bong.

This opening is not just a fun action scene. It sets up the whole movie's big idea: rich people get treated differently. Do-cheol only gets respect from the car dealer once he *looks* wealthy. That single idea comes back again and again.

### Meeting the Villain

Right after this case wraps up, Do-cheol is invited to an afterparty for a TV cop drama he had worked on as an advisor. Jo Tae-oh, the wealthy heir bankrolling the show, is there too. That is where the two men first meet, at a club, and Tae-oh is already showing his true colors, humiliating people around him just because he can. Do-cheol notices him immediately. Neither man likes what he sees in the other.

### The Attack on Bae

Driver Bae is a friend Do-cheol made during the smuggling case. Bae works for Tae-oh's company and is owed back pay. When Bae confronts Tae-oh about it, Tae-oh personally humiliates and beats him, right in front of Bae's young son. Bae ends up hospitalized in a coma. The company tries to cover it up, staging it to look like Bae attempted suicide.

This is the moment the story turns from fun to furious. Do-cheol does not buy the suicide story for one second.

### Tae-oh Fights Back

Once Do-cheol starts investigating, Tae-oh uses every tool he has:

- He pulls strings to get Do-cheol reassigned to a different case
- He has **Director Choi**, his top executive, try to bribe Do-cheol's wife to make the family back off (she refuses)
- He hires a contractor named Jeon to get rid of Do-cheol permanently

The murder attempt fails, though another detective on the team gets hurt. But it backfires badly for Tae-oh. The attack gives Do-cheol's team enough evidence to finally get official approval to investigate Tae-oh directly.

### The Company Sacrifices Someone

Here is where the story gets extra sharp. Once the walls start closing in, Tae-oh's own father, the company chairman, decides someone has to take the fall. He picks Director Choi, not his son. Money and blood matter more than loyalty in this world.

Meanwhile, Do-cheol proves that Bae's coma was no accident and no suicide attempt. It happened because Tae-oh beat him, and the whole scene was staged to hide that fact.

### The Chase and the Fight

Feeling the walls close in, Tae-oh tries to flee the country, planning an escape to Singapore. Do-cheol will not let that happen.

What follows is the movie's biggest set piece: a wild car chase through the streets of Seoul, ending in a brutal, bare-knuckle fistfight between Do-cheol and Tae-oh in Myeongdong, one of Seoul's busiest downtown areas. No more disguises. No more lawyers. No more hiding behind money. Just two men and a street full of witnesses.

Do-cheol takes a beating but refuses to quit. In the end, he wins. Tae-oh is arrested, right there in public, in front of the crowd he always assumed would look the other way.

### The Aftermath

Both Tae-oh and Director Choi end up sentenced to prison. Do-cheol recovers in the hospital from his injuries, but he is already looking ahead, ready to get back to work. In the end, it is Do-cheol's own persistence, his team's support, and the official investigation they fought to get approved that finally bring Tae-oh down.

## What the Ending Really Means

The final fight is filmed in a very public place on purpose. For the whole movie, Tae-oh has hidden behind money, lawyers, and family power. The fight strips all of that away. It happens in broad daylight, downtown, where regular people can see everything.

That choice is not an accident. It fits the whole point of the movie. The scene can be read as suggesting that unchecked power becomes vulnerable once it is dragged out into the open, where it can no longer hide.

The opening disguise scene and the closing street fight are mirror images of each other. In the beginning, Do-cheol has to pretend to be rich just to get basic respect. By the end, he does not need to pretend anything. He wins as exactly who he is.

---

## Why This Story Hit So Hard in Korea

Veteran arrived at a moment when stories about "gapjil," the misuse of power by the wealthy and privileged over ordinary workers, were already a hot topic of public conversation in South Korea. Director Ryoo Seung-wan himself said in interviews that he could not fully explain the film's massive success, but he believed audiences enjoyed watching an "imaginary hero" win against rich, powerful people in a way that rarely happens in real life. Its treatment of chaebol abuse likely contributed to the film's strong resonance with Korean audiences, on top of it being a well-made action comedy in its own right.

---

## Behind the Fight Scene

That final Myeongdong brawl was not a small effort. It was the very last scene the crew filmed during production, and it reportedly used around 100 background extras to fill out the street. The scale of the shoot suggests that considerable production effort went into getting that final showdown right.
