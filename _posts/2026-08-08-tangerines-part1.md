> *A personal note from the Editor.*

Out of every K-drama I watched in 2025, one has stayed with me the longest: **When Life Gives You Tangerines**. Its Korean title, "폭싹 속았수다," is actually Jeju Island dialect, not standard Korean. It is close to the standard Korean phrase "수고 많으셨습니다," but that everyday translation, "you worked so hard," undersells it. This is not the phrase you say to someone after they finish a task. It is something closer to "You've been through so much, and you've done so well," recognition, gratitude, comfort, and respect for everything a person has carried over the course of an entire life. That single phrase ends up capturing the whole spirit of the show.

I had not cried watching a drama in years. This one got me more than once. It shows Korea's poorer, harder decades honestly, without softening them, but it also finds real warmth, humor, and love inside all that hardship. It clearly struck a chord well beyond Korea too, becoming one of the most-watched non-English shows on Netflix that spring.

Today, let's introduce this one properly, with no spoilers.

---

## The Story (No Spoilers, Promise)

The story follows **Oh Ae-sun**, a spirited, headstrong girl from Jeju Island, and **Yang Gwan-sik**, the steady, quiet young man who has loved her for as long as he can remember. Beginning in 1960s Jeju, the series follows their lives across roughly five decades, expanding from Jeju to Seoul and Busan as their family story unfolds.

That is the setup, kept intentionally simple. This is a slow, generational family story, not a plot-twist thriller, and it earns every bit of its emotion honestly.

---

## Why This Show Feels Different

One creative choice makes this show especially memorable: **IU** plays two roles across the timeline, not one. She portrays both Ae-sun, the mother, in her younger years, and later, Ae-sun and Gwan-sik's grown daughter, Geum-myeong. Watching one actress carry both a mother's story and her daughter's story adds a quiet, powerful thread connecting the two generations.

The show comes from a strong creative pedigree. Director **Kim Won-seok** previously directed *My Mister* and *Misaeng*, two dramas already well known for their patient, deeply human storytelling. Writer **Lim Sang-choon**, known for *When the Camellia Blooms*, brings the same gift for finding humor and tenderness inside difficult lives. Together, they built something that feels less like a typical romance drama and more like a family history, told with real care.

## A Hit at Home and Abroad

When Life Gives You Tangerines premiered on Netflix on March 7, 2025, releasing four episodes a week across 16 total episodes. It quickly topped Netflix's TV show rankings in multiple countries and landed in Netflix's own weekly Global Top 10 for non-English series.

It also earned major recognition at home, winning **Best Drama** and **Best Screenplay** at the 61st Baeksang Arts Awards, South Korea's most prestigious television and film honors, along with supporting acting awards for Choi Dae-hoon and Yeom Hye-ran.
