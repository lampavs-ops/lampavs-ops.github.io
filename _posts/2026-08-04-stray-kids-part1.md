## Not Picked One by One. Picked as a Team.

Many televised idol survival shows pit trainees against one another, with only the "winners" earning a spot. Stray Kids used a notably different format.

In 2017, JYP Entertainment launched a reality survival show, also called *Stray Kids*, to introduce a new boy group. The first episode compared a male trainee project team, assembled by member Bang Chan, against a female trainee team. Once the male team was chosen as JYP's next debut project, the nine of them faced a series of missions built around one central question: could all nine actually debut together?

That team-first idea came from an unusual source. Member **Bang Chan** had already been training at JYP for years and personally helped choose several of his future groupmates before the show even began, and even named the group and designed its logo. In other words, before the cameras rolled, the "captain" was already picking his own crew.

Along the way, two members, Lee Know and Felix, were actually eliminated during the show. But in the finale, both were reinstated, confirming all nine trainees for the debut lineup.

## What "Stray Kids" Actually Means

The name is not random. It expresses the idea of young people straying from a prescribed path and pursuing their own direction, breaking away from convention to carve out an identity on their own terms.

That theme runs through the group's whole identity. Members **Bang Chan, Changbin, and Han** had already formed a pre-debut hip-hop and production trio called **3RACHA** before Stray Kids came together. That trio went on to become the group's principal production unit, writing and producing a large portion of Stray Kids' own music. Extensive, sustained member participation in songwriting and production became one of the group's defining features from day one.

---

## Debut: District 9 and a Dark, Defiant Sound

Stray Kids held their debut showcase in Seoul on March 25, 2018. Their first mini album, *I Am Not*, and its title track, "District 9," were officially released the next day, March 26. Before that, the survival show itself had already introduced the group's rougher, moodier sound: the music video for pre-debut single "Hellevator" was unveiled shortly before the program premiered, with the digital single released on November 1, 2017, while the show was still airing.

Right from the start, the group leaned into intense, genre-mixing production: heavy bass, industrial synths, and lyrics about pressure, rebellion, and self-worth. It was a bold choice for a rookie group, and it set Stray Kids apart from softer, more polished debuts happening around them.

## The Lineup, Then and Now

Stray Kids debuted with nine members. In October 2019, JYP Entertainment announced that Woojin had left the group due to personal circumstances and that his exclusive contract had been terminated. Stray Kids has continued as an eight-member group ever since:

- **Bang Chan** — leader, producer
- **Lee Know**
- **Changbin** — producer
- **Hyunjin**
- **Han** — producer
- **Felix**
- **Seungmin**
- **I.N**

## Why the "Team, Not Competition" Format Mattered

Korean survival shows are usually built around elimination and rivalry. Trainees are pitted against each other, ranked weekly, and told only the top scorers will debut, a format that has drawn real criticism over the years for the stress it puts on very young trainees competing against people they live and train with every day.

Stray Kids' show flipped that. Bang Chan was given the job of building a team, not winning a contest, and the show's central question was whether the group could get all nine members to debut together, not who would be cut. That difference shaped the group's identity from day one. Watching Korean idol culture from the inside, I think that origin story is a big part of why Stray Kids' self-producing, collective identity feels so genuine rather than like a marketing angle. The "team over competition" idea was baked into how they were built, not added afterward.
