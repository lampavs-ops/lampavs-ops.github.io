## Today's Comeback: THIS & THAT

Stray Kids just released their tenth Korean mini album, *THIS & THAT*, at 1 p.m. KST today, their first major Korean comeback since their record-breaking release "DO IT" late last year. The eight-track EP was once again fully self-produced by 3RACHA, and it takes the group in a noticeably calmer direction than some of their past title tracks.

At the album's press conference the day before release, member Changbin explained the shift: this time, the group wanted to sound "a little more chill and relaxed," trading some of their usual rougher edges for a more approachable sound while keeping their confidence intact. Member Seungmin added that despite years of success, the group still feels hungry to reach fans they have not met yet.

The comeback lands in the middle of an already busy year on the road, with the group performing new material for massive crowds even before finishing their current world tour.

## Still on the Road: The RUN IT World Tour

Stray Kids launched the *RUN IT World Tour* in Seoul on July 25, 2026, following the June 24 release of the pre-release single "Run It." Later this month, they are scheduled to bring the tour to MUFG Stadium (Japan National Stadium) in Tokyo on August 29–30, becoming the first foreign male act to headline the venue. Separately from the tour, the group is also set to appear at SBS Gayo Daejeon Summer at KINTEX and the TMElive International Music Awards (TIMA) at Kai Tak Main Stadium in Hong Kong. In September, they are also scheduled to appear at Rock in Rio in Brazil, one of the largest music festivals in the world.

It is a packed schedule for a group already holding some of the biggest touring records in K-pop history, and it shows no sign of slowing down.

## The Question Every Fan Is Asking: What About the Military?

Every male South Korean citizen is required to complete mandatory military service, and Stray Kids is not exempt from that reality. Bang Chan and Felix, who hold Australian citizenship, are not subject to Korea's mandatory service. But for the group's other six Korean members, military-service obligations are expected to become relevant in the coming years; their individual service classifications and dates have not been announced.

At this week's press conference, Lee Know, the oldest Korean member of the group, addressed the topic directly, saying he sees fulfilling military service as a natural responsibility and that he will carry out his duty faithfully when called. The members have not announced specific enlistment dates for anyone, but when asked about the group's future, they were clear on one point: Stray Kids will come back together after their service is complete.

No official enlistment dates have been confirmed for any member as of this post. We will update this series if that changes.
