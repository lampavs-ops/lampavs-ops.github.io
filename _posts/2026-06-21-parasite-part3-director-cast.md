## 🎬 The Director's Signature: Staircases and Class

Bong Joon-ho did not invent the "class" theme. But he found a visual language for it that nobody else uses quite the same way. Watch any Bong Joon-ho film closely and you will notice stairs, floors, and levels showing up again and again. In Parasite, the Kim family lives in a semi-basement apartment, low to the street. The Park family lives high on a hill, with a long staircase leading up to their front door. Characters are always walking up or down. The camera makes sure you feel it every time.

This is not new for Bong Joon-ho. In Snowpiercer, an entire movie takes place inside a single train, and the whole plot is about pushing forward from the poor cars in the back to the rich cars at the front. Parasite took that same idea and folded it into one house instead of one train. It is part of why critics call his films "vertical," meaning the story moves up and down almost as much as it moves forward.

## 🏆 What Happened After the Oscars

Winning four Academy Awards, including Best Picture, changed the entire cast's career overnight. Song Kang-ho, already a legend in Korea, went on to win Best Actor at the Cannes Film Festival in 2022 for a different movie, Broker, becoming the first Korean actor ever to win that award. Choi Woo-shik kept working steadily in both film and television. Park So-dam took a break from acting for health reasons and later returned to the screen.

The most bittersweet story belongs to Lee Sun-kyun, who played Mr. Park. He continued to build a strong career in Korean cinema and television after Parasite, and his death in December 2023 was mourned across the Korean film industry as the loss of one of its most respected actors.

Bong Joon-ho himself did not slow down. He went on to direct Mickey 17, an English-language science fiction film, proving that the director who made a basement in Seoul famous worldwide was just getting started.
