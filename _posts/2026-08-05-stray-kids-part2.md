## The Early Years: Finding Their Sound

After their 2018 debut, Stray Kids spent their first couple of years building an identity almost entirely through their own songwriting. Their *Clé* album series in 2019, including the title track "Miroh," pushed their sound into bigger, brighter territory while keeping the intensity that made them stand out. It was a period of steady growth rather than overnight fame, the kind of groundwork that pays off later.

Their breakthrough accelerated in 2020 with "God's Menu," the title track from their first full-length album *GO生 (GO LIVE)*, followed in September by "Back Door" from its repackaged album *IN生 (IN LIFE)*. *TIME* included *IN LIFE* among its Best K-Pop Songs and Albums of 2020, a notable honor for a K-pop release at that point.

---

## 2021: A Turning Point, Built on a Hard Year

2021 tested the group in a real way. Early in the year, member Hyunjin went on hiatus following a school bullying controversy from his middle school years, which we covered in Part 1. For several months, Stray Kids performed and promoted as a seven-member group, including their run on the competitive show *Kingdom: Legendary War*.

Despite performing without a full lineup, Stray Kids ended up winning *Kingdom: Legendary War*, beating out several already-established groups in a high-profile televised competition. Their performances during the show, including a reworked cover of a BLACKPINK song, drew huge attention online. Hyunjin rejoined the group that summer, and the eight members released their album *NOEASY* together in August, led by the title track "Thunderous."

That mix of hardship and comeback became a defining moment for the group. It showed how strongly the group's music, with 3RACHA at the core of its songwriting and production, could connect with a mainstream audience.

## Breaking Billboard, Again and Again

In 2022, Stray Kids signed with Republic Records for their U.S. promotions, and released *ODDINARY*, led by the single "Maniac." That album became their first ever Billboard 200 number-one, a massive milestone for a group that had built its identity around unusually extensive member involvement in songwriting and production.

What happened next was almost unheard of. The streak continued with *MAXIDENT*, ★★★★★ (5-STAR), 樂-STAR (ROCK-STAR), *ATE*, *HOP*, *KARMA*, and, in late 2025, *SKZ IT TAPE: DO IT*. *DO IT* gave Stray Kids their eighth consecutive Billboard 200 number-one, making them the first act in the chart's nearly 70-year history to have all of their first eight charting albums debut at the top, surpassing the previous record of five straight debuts held by DMX.

Along the way, the group also picked up major recognition at events like the Billboard Music Awards, and singles including "God's Menu," "S-Class," and "Case 143" have earned RIAA certifications in the United States, a sign of how far their reach had grown beyond Korea.

---

## From Arenas to Stadiums

Stray Kids' live shows grew right alongside their chart success. Their early tours played concert halls, but by the 2022–23 *MANIAC World Tour* they had moved into arenas across Asia and North America, with stadium encore dates following, including their first U.S. stadium show in Los Angeles. By their *dominATE World Tour*, which ran from August 2024 into late 2025, stadiums had become the centerpiece of the group's international touring, spanning Asia, Australia, Latin America, North America, and Europe and becoming one of the highest-grossing concert tours in K-pop history. They also became one of the first K-pop boy groups to headline major international festivals like Lollapalooza and BST Hyde Park.

From a rookie group once known mainly for their intensity and DIY spirit, Stray Kids had become one of the defining acts of their generation, in any genre.
