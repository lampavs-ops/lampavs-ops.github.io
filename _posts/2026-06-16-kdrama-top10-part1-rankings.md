## Why We Do Not Rank These Against Each Other

Netflix's own weekly "Top 10" charts, the ones you see on the app's homepage, are based on a point system weighted toward first-week views. Its twice-yearly Engagement Reports are different: they total up viewing hours across a fixed six-month window. And when Netflix announces a show broke "the record," that record is often measured over a specific stretch, like a title's first 28 days, not lifetime cumulative viewing. Those are three different rulers.

A slow-burn family drama with 16 hour-long episodes also racks up more total hours per viewer than a tense eight-episode thriller, even before you get into which reporting window each one happened to be measured in. Put a "first 28 days" record next to a "first half of 2024" total next to a "2025 peak window" estimate, and you get numbers that look comparable but are not actually measuring the same thing. That is why the table below is not sorted by hours. It is sorted by release date, and every figure is labeled with exactly what it measures.

> ⚠️ **Why these numbers cannot be stacked into one ranking:** Netflix has used at least three different counting methods over the years. Through mid-2023, its publicized records (used for Squid Game S1, All of Us Are Dead, The Glory, and Attorney Woo above) were based on a "household hours" metric measured over a show's first 28 days. In June 2023, Netflix switched its official Top 10 methodology to "hours viewed" over a rolling 91-day window counted per subscriber. Separately, its twice-yearly Engagement Reports (used for Queen of Tears above) total hours across a fixed six-month calendar window, a third, different measure. A number from one system is not directly comparable to a number from another, which is exactly why this list is ordered by release date instead of by hours.

## 🌍 Why Does the World Love K-Dramas?

- **Short and complete** — Most are 16 episodes. No filler. No years of waiting.
- **High production value** — Cinematography, OSTs, and costumes that rival Hollywood.
- **Real emotions** — K-dramas are not afraid to make you cry.
- **Strong characters** — Women and underdogs drive these stories.
- **Netflix's global reach** — Subtitles in 30+ languages means anyone can watch.

## What Global Audiences Latch Onto vs. What Koreans Talk About

Here is something that does not always come through in a straight ranking table: the reason a drama tops this list globally is often not the same reason it mattered inside Korea.

Take **Extraordinary Attorney Woo**. Internationally, most conversation around it centered on the whales — Woo Young-woo's love of them became a genuine meme. Inside Korea, the bigger conversation was about workplace accommodation for autistic employees, and whether Korean companies were actually prepared to hire someone like her in real life. The show landed differently depending on which side of that conversation you were standing on.

Or take **The Glory**. Global audiences mostly talked about the slow-burn revenge plot and Song Hye-kyo's performance. In Korea, the show reopened a much rawer, more specific conversation about school bullying, because Kim Eun-sook wrote it after researching real cases, and several real-life incidents resurfaced in the news cycle around the same time the show aired. It was not just a revenge fantasy here. It was closer to a mirror.

That gap between "why the world watched it" and "why Korea couldn't stop talking about it" is a big part of what makes each of these shows worth a closer look. The hours-watched number gets you in the door. It rarely tells you why a show actually stuck.

## Crash Landing on You (사랑의 불시착)

![Crash Landing on You](/assets/img/posts/kdrama-crash-landing.jpg)
*Crash Landing on You — tvN / Netflix (2019–2020)*

> 🎬 [**▶ Watch 'Crash Landing on You' Official Trailer on YouTube**](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Crash+Landing+on+You+Official+Trailer+Netflix)

### 📊 ~370,000,000 hours watched

This is the drama that started everything.

Before Squid Game. Before Queen of Tears. Before the world knew what a K-drama was — there was Crash Landing on You.

A South Korean heiress goes paragliding. A sudden storm sends her crashing across the border — into North Korea. A North Korean army officer finds her. Hides her. Protects her. And then, slowly, impossibly — they fall in love.

The premise sounds like a joke. But **Hyun Bin and Son Ye-jin** made it feel like the most real love story in the world. CLOY held the highest tvN drama rating record for **five years** — from 2020 until Queen of Tears finally broke it in 2024.

And then — the actors fell in love in real life too. They married in March 2022.

| **Genre** | Romantic Drama |

| **Peak Rating** | 21.7% — tvN record (held 2020–2024) |

### ✨ Why It Hit So Hard

CLOY worked because it was warm. North Korea as a setting was tense — but the drama chose to show humanity instead of politics. The North Korean soldiers were funny, kind, and loyal. The love was slow and earned.

It also came out at exactly the right moment — early 2020, just before COVID lockdowns. Millions of people discovered it while stuck at home, and it spread like wildfire.

Vincenzo Cassano is Korean-Italian. He was adopted by an Italian family as a child and grew up to become a mafia consigliere. Now he is back in Korea — to retrieve hidden gold. But he gets tangled in something bigger: a corrupt corporation that is destroying innocent people.

So he fights back. With style. With violence. With an entire building of ridiculous tenants who become his unlikely family.

**Song Joong-ki** plays Vincenzo with effortless cool. The show blends crime thriller, dark comedy, and K-drama warmth in a combination no drama had achieved before. It was ranked **#4 on Forbes' list of most-viewed Korean series on Netflix in 2021**.

## Squid Game (오징어 게임) Season 1

![Squid Game Season 1](/assets/img/posts/kdrama-squid-game-s1.jpg)
*Squid Game Season 1 — Netflix (2021)*

> 🎬 [**▶ Watch 'Squid Game Season 1' Official Trailer on YouTube**](https://www.youtube.com/results?search_query=Squid+Game+Season+1+Official+Trailer+Netflix)

### 📊 1,650,450,000 hours watched

No TV show in history has come close to this on Netflix.

Squid Game debuted on September 17, 2021. Within 28 days, over **111 million households** had watched it. It hit #1 in **94 countries** simultaneously. Netflix called it the most successful series launch in their entire history.

The story is simple and terrifying. 456 people buried in debt are invited to play children's games. Losing means death. The prize? ₩45.6 billion (about $33 million USD).

Creator **Hwang Dong-hyuk** spent 10 years trying to get this made. Studios said it was too dark. Too weird. Too Korean. Then Netflix said yes.

The rest is history.

### ✨ Why It Hit So Hard

The games are familiar. Red Light Green Light. Tug of War. Marbles. Everyone knows them from childhood.

But watching adults play them for their lives — with real desperation, real love, real betrayal — turned nostalgia into nightmare. It worked in every language, every culture.

> 💡 **Fun fact:** The "Red Light, Green Light" doll became one of the most recognized pop culture symbols of 2021. Real-size replicas appeared in cities across Asia and Europe. Fans lined up for hours just to take photos with her.

Set on **Jeju Island** across multiple decades, this drama follows two people — from childhood through old age. Through poverty. Through love. Through hardship and survival and the slow, quiet weight of a whole life lived together.

### ✨ Why It Hit So Hard

IU has always been beloved in Korea — as a singer and actress. But this role gave her the kind of generational performance that people will talk about for decades. Paired with Park Bo-gum's quiet warmth, the drama became something rare: a show that felt like life itself.

> 💡 **Fun fact:** "폭싹 속았수다" is a Jeju dialect phrase that means "you have worked so hard" or "you have truly suffered a great deal" — a warm expression of acknowledgment for a life of hardship. It is not related to the standard Korean word *속다* (to be fooled), despite the similar spelling. The title is a quiet tribute to the exhausting, beautiful lives the characters lived.

### 🇰🇷 Why Korea Cried Over a Jeju Dialect Title

*When Life Gives You Tangerines'* Korean title, "폭싹 속았수다," is written entirely in Jeju dialect (제주어) — a regional way of speaking that mainland Korean audiences themselves often need subtitles for. That choice was deliberate. The show is set on Jeju Island, and using the island's own dialect for the title, instead of translating it into standard Korean, was the production signaling from the very first frame that this was a story rooted in a specific place and a specific way of life, not a generic period drama.

That's a layer that Netflix's English title, "When Life Gives You Tangerines," can't fully carry — it lands as a warm idiom in English, but it loses the specific weight of a phrase that's really only spoken by Jeju grandmothers describing a hard life, well lived. Knowing that context doesn't change how the ending hits. It just explains why so many Korean viewers said the title alone made them tear up before the story even started.
